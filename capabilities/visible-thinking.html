Why it matters for learning

A response can reveal more than a score. A class view, a well-chosen example and a prompt to check understanding help the teacher decide where to spend attention.

How I build it

  • I bring the response close to the teacher’s decision, rather than reducing a learner to a label.
  • Signals prompt professional judgement. Fictional class views illustrate the workflow; they are not evidence of live student monitoring.

Student Thinking Grid

See responses together and filter by Needs review, Reviewed or Waiting. Open an answer to compare it with the model and a prepared criterion-based suggestion.

Teacher control. The teacher can approve or change the mark and edit the feedback. The demo uses twelve numbered fictional learners.

Open a response, inspect its evidence, and approve or change the feedback.

Show an answer on the projector

Choose a response to discuss with the whole class. The projector shows the question and an anonymous answer, without teacher-only assessment details.

Teacher control. The teacher chooses the example and leads the discussion. The visible answer is fictional.

Inspect the anonymous answer; return to Thinking grid to choose a different one.

A moment to check understanding

The class overview flags a sample response that judges river health from appearance alone. Its evidence and conversation give the teacher something concrete to investigate.

Teacher control. The signal is a prepared, rule-based prompt. The teacher reads the work before deciding what support to offer.

Review the flagged learner and compare the draft with the evidence.

Source: Maestro

Lesson cards and exit reflection

Build the lesson as Notice, Explain, Check and Reflect cards. Edit a question and its model answer, then launch the card to gather responses.

Teacher control. The teacher chooses the prompt and model answer. Responses to edited cards use manual marking in the demo; prepared AI suggestions belong to the supplied evaporation example.

Edit the reflection prompt and model answer, save the card, then launch it.

See the class evidence together

Bring the teams’ repeated trials into a class plot. Compare panel angles, filter a team’s results and use the pattern to frame a discussion.

Teacher control. The teacher interprets the evidence with the class. This is a local sample dashboard with fictional trials, not a feed from connected student devices.

Filter by team, inspect the means and compare them with the class plot.
