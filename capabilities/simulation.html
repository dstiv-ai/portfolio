Why it matters for learning

A model makes an idea testable. Moving from prediction to observation, reflection and redesign gives Science, Mathematics and Technology a shared purpose.

How I build it

  • I keep model assumptions and fictional measurements visible. A simulation is an explanation to test, not a guarantee of real-world performance.
  • Students are inquirers and creators: they make a prediction, interpret a result and justify their next design decision.

Features in practice

Source: Water Rocket Lab

Design, predict, compare

Choose water, pressure, mass and fins; record a prediction; inspect the calculated flight; then compare it with clearly labelled fictional launch data.

Teacher control. The teacher frames the fair test and interprets the model’s limits. The sample launch is not a validation result or a physical safety rating.

Choose a prediction, calculate the flight, then compare and write an evidence note.

Source: Solar Car STEM Challenge

Log a simulated trial

Run a short sensor playback, record a distance and time, validate the measurements and save the trial into the results.

Teacher control. Readings are generated locally; no micro:bit is connected. The class data and teams are fictional.

Run a simulated trial, then save it and see how the results change.

Source: Solar Car STEM Challenge

Turn results into a design decision

Use plotted repeated trials and calculated means to write an evidence response, then develop a design improvement and a plan to test it.

Teacher control. Students must explain what the evidence supports. Mean speed comes from distance divided by time; the fixed-light sample does not predict every real setup.

Read the means and plot, edit the evidence response, then open Engineer.

Source: MaqueenLab

Connect movement, blocks and sensors

Edit a focused set of blocks, run the simulated robot and compare the movement with its sensor rule. Record a drive to connect an action with code.

Teacher control. The teacher can use the model before hardware is available. It is a focused teaching editor, not the full MakeCode environment, and does not flash a micro:bit.

Change the distance rule, run the blocks, or record a short drive and inspect the code.

Try it

Source: Race Lab

Design a car before building it

Choose the car’s body, motor, gearing, wheels and battery. Use the predicted race time and speed and acceleration graphs to reason about the design.

Teacher control. A model prediction needs comparison with a real race. The teacher frames the test and judges what conclusions the evidence supports.
