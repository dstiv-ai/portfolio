Why it matters for learning

Feedback helps when learners can connect their work to a clear goal and a next action. The design should make the evidence, the judgement and the next step easy to compare.

How I build it

  • Criteria belong to the teacher. Suggested marks are starting points for review.
  • Approval and release are deliberate actions. The public AI suggestions are prepared examples, not live marking of visitor writing.

Features in practice

Source: Aissess

Rubric builder

Edit four criteria and four performance levels, save the rubric, and make expectations explicit before reviewing work.

Teacher control. The teacher writes the criteria. Changing the rubric does not recalculate the demo’s prepared marking example.

Open a criterion, change its wording and save the rubric.

Source: Aissess

Review, approve and release feedback

Put a sample response beside criterion-level suggestions. Adjust the score and feedback, approve the decision, then release it to the student preview.

Teacher control. Editing an approved decision requires approval again. The prepared suggestion applies only to the supplied sample response.

Change the suggested score or feedback; approve your decision before releasing it.

Try it

Source: Aissess

Live quiz: review before release

A three-question quiz retains answers, checks them against a fixed key and gives a next step based on the choices made.

Teacher control. The teacher reviews and approves the quiz feedback before student preview can see it. The demo shows one browser, not a connected class.

Answer the three questions, then review, approve and release the feedback.

Try it

Source: Engineering practice papers

Immediate answer explanations

Check a multiple-choice answer, read why it is right or wrong, and try again. Written examples show how criteria lead to feedback.

Teacher control. Multiple choice is checked locally. Custom written answers receive a model and criteria for self-checking, not an invented AI mark.

Try again with another answer, or move to a written question to compare a prepared example.
