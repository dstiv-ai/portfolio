Why it matters for learning

Shared equipment needs clear decisions, visible status and a record of what happened. Good stewardship makes resources easier to use responsibly and problems easier to resolve.

How I build it

  • I make approvals, material limits and recovery decisions visible to the teacher.
  • The demo uses fictional jobs and scripted printer states. It demonstrates the workflow, not hardware reliability, physical safety or production readiness.

Review a print before approving it

Review a prepared design, its estimated time and material use. Approve it or return it for revision before supervised release.

Teacher control. The teacher makes the decision. Sample designs replace real uploads and slicing in this demonstration.

Open the pending job and choose an approval or revision decision.

Track material allowances

Approval reserves the learner’s allowance. Stopping a print refunds the reservation once, while plastic already used remains recorded against stock.

Teacher control. The material limits are explicit. All learners, quantities and jobs in the demo are fictional.

Inspect the allowances, then approve a job in Review and return to see the change.

Try it

Source: PrintLab3D

Watchdog alert, teacher decision

A scripted watchdog example pauses a sample print. The teacher inspects the observation and decides whether to resume or stop it.

Teacher control. The alert is prepared and labelled. It is not live AI detection and does not operate real printers.

Select the printing job and trigger the scripted failure example; decide what to do next.

Try it

Source: PrintLab3D

Bookings and an activity trail

Check booking conflicts and follow the activity trail of approvals and printer decisions.

Teacher control. The teacher can trace the sample workflow. There are no connected users or shared real-time bookings.

Try booking an occupied slot, then inspect Activity for recorded decisions.
